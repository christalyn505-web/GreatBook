The End

Thank you for reading. People treat fables like they are only for kids, but they are actually where the great books started, everything after them was built on top.

When people say “great books,” they usually picture something thick and serious sitting on a high shelf. But none of those books came out of nowhere. Long before anyone was writing novels, people were telling fables out loud, short stories about animals who talk, where a carabao finds out what bayanihan actually means, or an eagle learns to wait for the right wind instead of fighting the storm. That is where a lot of it starts. The fables in this collection belong to that same line. They are not the warm-up act before real literature; they are part of the floor the rest of it was built on.

Take fables out and a lot of what we read now would fall apart, or at least get a lot cruder. You would still have events, this happened, then that happened, but not the habit of a story meaning something underneath. Fables are what trained stories to carry a moral at all, and honestly that is the only reason we can argue about whether a character was right or wrong. It is also why they still matter outside of class. The things people argue about in the news, helping someone when there is nothing in it for you, wanting the life somebody else has, being patient when rushing is easier, how you treat a person you have decided is smaller than you, those are the exact arguments these stories are already holding. A fable just makes one small enough to pick up and turn around in your hands.
